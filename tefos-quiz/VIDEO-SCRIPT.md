# Video Demonstration Script

### "The Moment After the Chapter"

**Purpose:** Convince a principal or HQ board in under two minutes, then hand them the three asks.
**Tone:** Quiet, confident, then lifting. Not hype. The power is in the reveal, not the adjectives.
**Runtime:** 2 minutes 15 seconds master · 60-second cut · 30-second social cut
**Format:** 16:9, 4K or 1080p. Real locations. Real phones. Real students where consent allows.

---

## The one-line concept

> Every school measures attendance, grades, and fees.
> **Nobody measures the forty minutes a year a student spends finishing a chapter.**
> We built the channel for that moment — and it was already on the desk.

## Sound and music

| Cue | Time | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Bell + page turn | 0:00 | Dry, close-mic'd, real. No music under it. |
| Silence builds | 0:04–0:18 | A single sustained low tone enters around 0:16. Felt more than heard. |
| **The turn** | 0:28 | A soft piano note on the stack of notebooks. The mix opens. This is the hinge of the film — hold the silence one beat longer than feels comfortable. |
| Rising bed | 0:36–1:16 | Minimal, rhythmic, unhurried. Think marimba and soft strings. **Nothing triumphant yet.** |
| Lift | 1:16 | On the result screen. This is where the music earns its energy. |
| **Peak** | 1:52–2:08 | Full. Warm, human, hopeful. Real instruments — this is a school, not a tech startup. |
| Resolve | 2:08–2:20 | Strip back to a single note. Let the final line land in near-silence. |
| Stinger | 2:20 | Logo sting. Short. Confident. No jingle. |

**Rule: no sound effect that isn't diegetic.** If it happened in a real classroom, it can be in the film. No whooshes, no digital zaps, no UI clicks.

## B-roll shot list

Everything here can be filmed in one afternoon with a phone and one person who is allowed to be in a classroom.

1. **The stack** — 200+ identical notebooks, bound and wrapped, in a corridor. Shoot it low and slow. This is the hero image.
2. **The closing hand** — a student shuts a Physics notebook. Film it ten times and take the fourth. Weight matters.
3. **The cover** — one notebook face-up, raking light across the QR. This shot carries the film.
4. **The scan** — over-the-shoulder, real phone, real browser. Do not fake the screen. Handheld is better than tripod here.
5. **Faces** — three to five students, working, natural light, no one performing for the camera. Consent forms signed; prefer a shot that does not require a recognisable face.
6. **The handover** — a bursary certificate or trophy being passed from one hand to another. Film it low, from the side, in focus on the hands.
7. **The corridor** — a wide, static shot of a full corridor at break. This is the "sixteen schools" shot.
8. **The crest** — on a wall, in light, alone. Hold for three seconds at the end.

**Do not film:** anyone upset, anyone being corrected, any noticeboard with a child's name on it, or any other school's materials. This is a film about opportunity, not about problem.

## Production notes

**The screen recording is the spine of the film and must be perfect.** Capture it on the actual device you intend to film on. Slow, deliberate taps — the pace of a student who has just finished a lesson, not of someone showing off a product. Leave the browser chrome visible. Seeing a real URL and a real 404-capable internet connection is more persuasive than a pristine render.

**Film the stack of notebooks the same day you film the ceremonies.** You cannot recreate a hundred wrapped notebooks in a week, and that image is doing more persuasive work than any line in this script.

**Get the three asks onto screen before you finish, not after.** The film is a commercial, not a teaser. A principal who watches two minutes and doesn't know what you want from them will compliment you warmly and do nothing.

**Have a hard cut ready to the 30-second vertical version before you start shooting.** It is the version that actually travels in a school WhatsApp group, and it is the version a principal forwards to a colleague at eleven at night.

**Consent.** Signed parental consent for any recognisable minor, in writing, before the shoot. If consent is not in hand, shoot hands, backs, and notebooks. The film is better for it — faces are the least interesting thing in any frame in this script.

**One take, no narration in the room.** The school sounds are the music. Record the voice-over separately, in a quiet room, and get close to the mic. The quietness of the read matters more than its polish.
